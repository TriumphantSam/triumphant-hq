# TriumphantTech: client demonstration and onboarding

Use the working business flows as evidence. Show the customer's problem, perform
one action, show its verified result, then explain how their business would use
it. Do not promise revenue, rankings, delivery times or platform approvals.

## What can be demonstrated now

| Flow | Working demonstration | Limit to explain |
| --- | --- | --- |
| AI assistant | Ask AI on a public service page; approved facts and source links; real enquiry handoff | Knowledge is limited to published business facts; security check required |
| Enquiries | Form → durable record → Google Sheet mirror → confirmation and owner email | Saved, provider accepted and inbox received are separate states |
| Appointments | Real availability, booking, email confirmation, cancellation and email reminders | SMS and WhatsApp remain pending; booking reserves a real consultation slot |
| Payment | Clearly labelled ₦100 Paystack test checkout → verification → receipt | No real money, delivered product or live billing; do not show a test receipt as revenue |
| Reviews | Same Google option for every rating; optional private feedback; permission-based invitation after completed service | Never submit a fabricated public review; do not suppress unhappy customers |
| Owner workspace | Real enquiries, bookings, private feedback, report schedule and draft approval | Customer records are private; financial and stock integrations are not connected |
| WhatsApp | Explain the planned company-number menu and handoff | Company number +234 909 009 0910 is awaiting Meta API connection; do not promise a bot reply today |

The public reporting demo deliberately uses sample data. Weekly writing drafts
are source-based templates requiring approval; they are not AI-written and do
not publish automatically. Production owner reports are scheduled for 08:00 WAT;
Monday covers the previous week. Scheduled email delivery must be checked when
the first report is due.

## Recording preparation

Use a clean browser with no unrelated tabs or notifications visible. Use an
email address you control for any submission. Explain email side effects before
asking a prospect to try a form. Prepare screenshots of verified results rather
than repeatedly sending test messages. Hide private customer information, API
keys, admin credentials and appointment management codes from recordings.

Complete human verification yourself. Never disable it for a recording. A
technical demonstrator should obtain the owner's approval before creating
bookings or sending test emails on their behalf.

## Sixty-second scripts

### 1. Website assistant: answer → source → human

- **0–10s:** Open `/services/seo`. “Visitors can ask a question without leaving
  the service they are considering.” Open **Ask AI**, verify and start.
- **10–30s:** Ask “Do you offer SEO in Lagos? Can you guarantee first place?”
  Show the factual answer, the SEO source and the refusal to guarantee rankings.
- **30–45s:** Ask “How much will my website cost?” Explain that project scope
  is needed; the assistant must not invent a fixed fee.
- **45–60s:** Choose **Talk to our team** and show the enquiry form. “For your
  business, we start with your approved services, FAQs and contact process.”

Do not submit the enquiry solely to finish the recording. If a live handoff is
needed, use an agreed test address and label the enquiry as a demonstration.

### 2. Lead capture: enquiry → business record

- **0–10s:** Open `/automation-lab/lead-capture`. Describe a business losing
  enquiries between its website and inbox.
- **10–25s:** Submit one agreed demonstration enquiry using an address you
  control. Explain that it sends real emails.
- **25–40s:** Show the saved reference and delivery states. Show only the
  corresponding test row in the Sheet, with other rows hidden.
- **40–55s:** Show the test acknowledgement and owner alert if received.
  If only provider acceptance is confirmed, say so.
- **55–60s:** “The same enquiry can become an appointment, quote or follow-up
  in your existing process.”

### 3. Booking: availability → confirmation → cancellation

- **0–10s:** Open `/automation-lab/booking`. “Customers choose from times the
  business can genuinely honour.”
- **10–25s:** Show the Lagos timezone, 30-minute consultation, working hours
  and available slots. This business uses weekdays 10am–4pm, a 15-minute buffer
  and 24 hours' notice.
- **25–40s:** Use an existing authorised test confirmation or make one agreed
  booking. Explain confirmation and email reminders at 24 hours and 2 hours.
- **40–55s:** Show cancellation with private codes hidden; cancel any agreed
  test booking and confirm that the slot is released.
- **55–60s:** “Your hours, appointment types and reminder channels are
  configured during setup. WhatsApp and SMS require their own activation.”

Never book a real client slot without agreement. Do not change minimum notice
or reminder timings just to make the demonstration look faster.

### 4. Payment: simulated checkout → verified receipt

- **0–10s:** Open `/automation-lab/payments` and point out **test mode**.
  “This is a ₦100 simulation. No real money is collected.”
- **10–30s:** With an agreed test email, open Paystack checkout and select its
  successful test option. Do not enter real card details.
- **30–45s:** Return to the website and choose **Check this payment**. Show the
  verified status, amount and reference.
- **45–55s:** Open the receipt. Show the business details and Lagos payment
  date; show an email only if receipt delivery was actually confirmed.
- **55–60s:** “For a live rollout, we agree the real products, fulfilment,
  invoice rules and payment-account configuration first.”

Leave demonstration reminders unchecked unless their sends are specifically
part of the agreed test. Existing approved test evidence can be reused without
creating another transaction or email.

### 5. Reviews: equal access → private improvement loop

- **0–15s:** Open `/automation-lab/reviews`. Select one star, then five stars.
  Show that both choices retain the same Google review link.
- **15–30s:** Show optional private feedback. “Everyone can share feedback;
  unhappy customers are not prevented from leaving a public review.”
- **30–45s:** Explain the separate booking permission and owner marking a
  completed service. Invitations are limited to one per customer per 90 days,
  with an opt-out.
- **45–60s:** “This helps you ask consistently after real service, without
  inventing reviews or offering a reward for a positive rating.”

Do not post a public Google review as a demonstration. Use the already verified
private test or obtain approval for another private feedback alert.

### 6. Reporting and drafts: owner visibility → approval

- **0–15s:** Open `/automation-lab/admin` and state “This public dashboard uses
  sample data.” Show the report and sample stock interaction.
- **15–30s:** Show how a writing draft is reviewed and approved without
  publishing. These are templates, not generated AI copy.
- **30–45s:** Explain that the protected owner workspace uses actual enquiries,
  bookings and feedback. Prefer a sanitised screenshot over showing real records.
- **45–60s:** “The daily report is connected to our operational records. Your
  sales and stock figures would come from your own connected system.”

Do not present sample stock alerts, test payments or disconnected revenue as
TriumphantTech's live financial performance.

### 7. WhatsApp: honest preview while approval is pending

- **0–15s:** Open `/automation-lab/whatsapp` and show the connection status.
- **15–35s:** Explain the intended LAB/SERVICES/PRICES/ORDER/BOOK flow and human
  handoff. The intended company number is +234 909 009 0910.
- **35–50s:** Show a working website booking or enquiry flow as the connected
  destination. Do not substitute a different API sender to stage a reply.
- **50–60s:** “The company-number API connection is awaiting Meta approval.
  We will record the live conversation after activation.”

This is a preview, not evidence of a currently working WhatsApp bot. Proactive
messages, templates, consent, STOP handling and coexistence must be verified
before the channel is offered as live.

## First client discovery

Start with one measurable bottleneck: missed enquiries, appointment no-shows,
manual payment confirmation, delayed reviews or disconnected owner reporting.
Ask about the current steps, approximate volume, responsible staff, existing
tools and the point where work gets lost. Agree what counts as a successful
pilot before quoting; do not manufacture a price or savings claim.

Collect approved services and FAQs, a real owner inbox, genuine booking hours,
the client's review link and any existing business-system integration details.
For WhatsApp, establish number ownership, current Business app use, existing
chats and Meta account access before discussing migration. For payments, agree
test-mode acceptance and an explicit live activation step.

Use delegated account access and provider secret settings. Each client's data,
credentials, webhook verification and recipients must be isolated. The current
TriumphantTech deployment is one business; it is not a multi-tenant client
platform. Do not add a client's contacts or transactions to this database.

## Handover evidence for a paid pilot

Record the approved scope, setup dependencies, test references and observed
results. Show storage separately from provider acceptance and actual delivery.
Document who handles enquiries, failed jobs, cancellations, refunds and opt-outs.
Provide owner access privately, a clear support route, and an agreed rollback or
pause process. Obtain approval before enabling live charges, outbound messaging
or publishing. Never auto-publish social drafts as part of a demonstration.
